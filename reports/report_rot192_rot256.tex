\title{\textbf{Three-dimensional MHD evolution of an ultramassive white dwarf\\
with differential rotation and a mixed field}\\[0.3em]
\large Results at $192^3$ and $256^3$ --- interim report}
\author{Rafael C.\ R.\ de Lima}
\date{2 August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We evolve a $2.005\,\Msun$ equilibrium with strong differential rotation and a
toroidal-dominated field in three-dimensional MHD, at two resolutions. The
central result is a separation: \textbf{the star survives and keeps its mass
while its ordered magnetic field is destroyed}. Over $12\,\mathrm{s}$ the
magnetic energy falls by $95\%$ and the toroidal-to-poloidal ratio drops from
$3\times10^5$ to $10$, while the mass varies by $0.42\%$ with no trend. The
instability responsible is non-axisymmetric, dominated by $m=1$, and grows in a
few Alfv\'en times. Its growth rate is \emph{not} converged: it rises by $47\%$
from $192^3$ to $256^3$, so the measured value is a lower bound. Two runs are
still in progress, the $192^3$ one already past $t = 22\,\mathrm{s}$.
\end{abstract}

%======================================================================
\section{What is being simulated}

\subsection{The configuration}

The object is a white dwarf of $M = 2.005\,\Msun$, that is \textbf{38\% above
the Chandrasekhar limit} for $\mu_e = 2$ ($1.456\,\Msun$). The excess is held up
by differential rotation, and that is the physical reason the star is so
flattened: $\Req = 3.917\times10^8$\,cm against $\Rpol = 1.501\times10^8$\,cm,
or $\Rpol/\Req = 0.383$.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Mass & $2.0052\,\Msun$ & $1.38\,M_{\mathrm{Ch}}$ \\
Central density & $3.0\times10^{9}$ g\,cm$^{-3}$ & \\
Radii & $\Req = 3.917\times10^8$ cm & $\Rpol = 1.501\times10^8$ cm \\
Rotation & $\Omega_c = 8.193$ rad\,s$^{-1}$ & $j$-constant law, $A = \Req$ \\
$T/|W|$ & $0.0993$ & mass-shedding parameter $0.469$ \\
Toroidal field & $\max|\Btor| = 3.23\times10^{13}$ G & $0.732\,B_c$ \\
Poloidal field & surface dipole $1.0\times10^{9}$ G & \\
Amplitude ratio & $B_t/B_p = 4234$ & $\Etor/E_{\mathrm{mag}} = 0.99999995$ \\
Timescales & $t_{\mathrm{dyn}} = 0.475$ s & $t_A = 2.383$ s, $P_{\mathrm{rot}} = 0.767$ s \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Properties of the initial model \texttt{rotating\_mixed.txt}.}
\end{table}

The rotation law is $j$-constant,
$\Omega(\varpi) = \Omega_c A^2/(A^2+\varpi^2)$ with $A = \Req$. It is the most
differential case of that family and leaves the star \emph{marginally} stable by
the Rayleigh criterion ($\mathrm{d}j/\mathrm{d}\varpi = 0$).

\subsection{The fields}

The field is \textbf{mixed but toroidal-dominated to one part in $10^7$ by
energy}. The poloidal component exists as a weak $10^9$\,G surface dipole;
integrated, $\Epol/|W| = 5.2\times10^{-10}$. This is the collaboration's
specification --- strong toroidal interior, weak exterior dipole --- and it is
precisely the combination that makes a nearly purely toroidal field
Tayler-unstable.

\subsection{Equation of state}

\texttt{ztwd}: a \textbf{zero-temperature, fully degenerate} electron gas with
$\mu_e = 2$. It is \textbf{barotropic}, $P = P(\rho)$: all temperature
derivatives vanish identically and the heat capacities are zero. Two
consequences follow, taken up in Sec.~\ref{sec:caveats} --- there is no thermal
reservoir to receive dissipated energy, and the stratification is neutral, with
no buoyancy to limit magnetic instabilities.

\subsection{Numerical method}

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Code:} \textsc{Castro} 26.07 on AMReX; ideal MHD with constrained
      transport, so div$\,\mathbf{B}$ is preserved by construction; HLLD Riemann
      solver; PPM off.
\item \textbf{Grid:} 3D Cartesian, single level --- \textsc{Castro}'s
      constrained transport does not support AMR. Box of $1.8\times10^9$\,cm per
      side, identical at both resolutions.
\item \textbf{Resolutions:} $192^3$ ($\mathrm{d}x = 9.375\times10^6$\,cm) and
      $256^3$ ($7.031\times10^6$\,cm).
\item \textbf{Gravity:} \texttt{PoissonGrav}, full 3D Poisson solve. Not
      monopole --- with $\Rpol/\Req = 0.38$ the quadrupole is large and a
      monopole approximation makes the star ring.
\item \textbf{Initial condition:} the axisymmetric model is built by
      Grad--Shafranov/SCF on a $385\times769$ $(\varpi,z)$ grid and interpolated
      onto the Cartesian mesh from the vector potential. The reconstruction
      retains $96.0\%$ of the peak $|\Btor|$ at $96^3$ and $98.9\%$ at $192^3$.
      The field enters in Heaviside--Lorentz units,
      $\mathbf{B}' = \mathbf{B}/\sqrt{4\pi}$.
\item \textbf{Robustness:} density floor $10^2$\,g\,cm$^{-3}$ against an ambient
      of $2.0\times10^4$; sponge between $10^4$ and $10^5$; velocity damping over
      the first $2\,t_{\mathrm{dyn}}$, targeted at the \emph{model's} velocity
      field rather than at zero.
\item \textbf{CFL:} $0.5$ at $192^3$; $0.3$ at $256^3$ from $t \simeq 4.8$\,s,
      needed to get through saturation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Diagnostics}

\textsc{Castro}'s native magnetic diagnostics (\texttt{emag\_density},
\texttt{etor\_density}, \texttt{Div\_B}) proved unusable: all three pack
face-centred components of different index types into a single cell-centred FAB
and read past the end of the data box, with \texttt{Div\_B} reaching
$3\times10^{146}$ where it should be $\sim0$. The components $B_x, B_y, B_z$ are
written correctly, and every number here was rebuilt from them with purpose-written
tools (\texttt{fbtbp}, \texttt{fmodes}, \texttt{fslice}), using
\[
\Btor = \frac{-y\,B_x + x\,B_y}{\varpi}, \qquad
B_{\mathrm{pol}}^2 = B_x^2 + B_y^2 + B_z^2 - \Btor^2 .
\]
Mass and radii are integrated over cells with $\rho > 10^5$\,g\,cm$^{-3}$, so
they refer to the \emph{star} and not to the box.

\section{Results}

\subsection{The star survives; the field does not}

This is the central result and it holds at both resolutions.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
& $t = 0$ & $t = 12$\,s & ratio \\
\midrule
Stellar mass ($\Msun$) & 2.00667 & 2.00160 & $0.9975$ \\
$\Etor$ (erg) & $6.035\times10^{49}$ & $2.947\times10^{48}$ & $\mathbf{0.049}$ \\
$E_{\mathrm{mag}}$ (erg) & $6.035\times10^{49}$ & $3.078\times10^{48}$ & $0.051$ \\
$\Etor/\Epol$ & $3.11\times10^{5}$ & $22.6$ & \\
$\max|\Btor|$ (G) & $3.196\times10^{13}$ & $3.539\times10^{13}$ & $1.11$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Initial and final state of the $192^3$ run. The mass is conserved to
$0.42\%$ in amplitude over the whole series, with no trend, while the magnetic
energy falls by a factor of 20.}
\end{table}

Note the last line: \textbf{the peak field does not fall --- it rises by 11\%}.
What disappears is the large-scale organisation, not the local strength.
Defining an effective volume by
$V_{\mathrm{eff}} = 8\pi \Etor/\max|\Btor|^2$, the fraction of the star occupied
by the toroidal field falls from $1.23\%$ to $0.066\%$: a factor of 18 in
concentration. The field becomes filamentary.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/field_sequence_192.png}
\caption{$192^3$. \textbf{Top:} equatorial cut $z=0$, where $\Btor$ lies in the
plane, so the streamlines of $(B_x,B_y)$ \emph{are} the toroidal field lines --
circles while the star is axisymmetric, and their departure from circles is the
instability. Colour is $\Btor$. \textbf{Bottom:} meridional cut $y=0$, where
$(B_x,B_z)$ \emph{is} the poloidal field and the lines are its loops. Colour is
$|\Bpol|$. Both maps start at the same $10^{10}$\,G, so the near-empty poloidal
panel at $t=0$ is the statement that the poloidal field is three decades below
the toroidal one. Dashed: $\rho = 10^6$\,g\,cm$^{-3}$. Same scales as
Fig.~\ref{fig:seq256}.}
\label{fig:seq192}
\end{figure}

Fig.~\ref{fig:seq192} shows the process. Seen face-on, the toroidal field starts
as concentric circles, develops spiral arms by $t \simeq 4$\,s with patches of
\emph{reversed} sign, and ends as interleaved bands of both signs -- the same
$m=1$ structure the mode decomposition measures, and the same filamentation the
volume-filling number gives. The poloidal field does the opposite: it starts as
a clean dipole carrying $10^{-7}$ of the magnetic energy, below the colour floor
entirely, and ends up filling the star with its lines tangled beyond any flux
function.

\subsection{Density, mass, radius and volume}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\textwidth]{figures/mass_radius_192.pdf}
\caption{$192^3$ to $t=12$\,s. (a) stellar mass against a $\pm1\%$ band; (b) the
three radii, with one grid cell drawn as a band around $\Rpol$; (c) volume;
(d) oblateness. The offset in $\Rpol/\Req$ at $t=0$ --- $0.425$ measured against
$0.383$ in the model --- is discretisation, not evolution: $\Rpol$ quantises onto
multiples of $\mathrm{d}x$ and $\Req$ is cut short by the density threshold
biting into a shallow equatorial profile.}
\label{fig:massradius}
\end{figure}

The mass stays between $1.99971$ and $2.00822\,\Msun$ --- $0.42\%$ in amplitude,
no trend --- and converges towards the model value, $2.00515$, as the ambient
material captured by the cut disperses. \textbf{The star loses no matter},
despite losing $95\%$ of its magnetic energy.

What it does instead is pulsate, hard: the volume swings by a factor of $3.46$
over the run, peaking at $2.51\,V_0$ at $t = 1.8$\,s. The mean density runs from
$1.31$ to $4.53\times10^{7}$\,g\,cm$^{-3}$. By $t = 12$\,s the star is
\emph{smaller} than it began ($0.74\,V_0$) but with half its initial central
density: it has become markedly \textbf{less centrally concentrated}, with
$\rho_c/\langle\rho\rangle$ falling from 91 to 34.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\textwidth]{figures/long_run_192.pdf}
\caption{$192^3$ extended to $t = 22$\,s (run in progress). (a) central density;
(b) axial angular momentum, with the two fitted braking rates. The dashed
vertical marks the restart at $t=12$\,s from \texttt{chk03436}; it is a restart,
not a discontinuity.}
\label{fig:long}
\end{figure}

Past the initial transient the pulsation \textbf{does not damp}: it locks into a
clean periodic mode (Fig.~\ref{fig:long}a) with
\[
P = 1.498\ \mathrm{s} = 3.15\,t_{\mathrm{dyn}} = 1.95\,P_{\mathrm{rot}},
\qquad \text{amplitude } \pm14.4\%\ \text{in } \rho_{\max},
\]
its envelope decaying with an $e$-folding near $140$\,s. Successive peaks:
$1.531$, $1.530$, $1.540$, $1.528$, $1.530$, $1.532$ ($\times10^9$).

\subsection{Evolution of the fields}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\textwidth]{figures/bt_bp_192.pdf}
\caption{$192^3$ to $t=12$\,s. (a) toroidal and poloidal energies, with the
$t=0$ numerical floor of $\Epol$ marked and the exponential fit to the growth
phase; (b) the two toroidal-to-poloidal ratios, in energy and in peak amplitude,
which saturate in different places; (c) peak field against the Landau critical
field.}
\label{fig:btbp}
\end{figure}

The field goes through three distinct phases (Fig.~\ref{fig:btbp}):

\paragraph{Growth, $t = 1.3$--$4.0$\,s.} The poloidal energy grows
exponentially, $\sigma = 1.369$\,s$^{-1}$ with $r = 0.994$, starting from
$1.94\times10^{44}$\,erg --- which is the interpolation floor, not physics. The
$e$-folding of the \emph{amplitude} is $1.46$\,s $= 0.61\,t_A$.

\paragraph{Saturation, $t \simeq 5.3$\,s.} $\Epol$ reaches
$1.88\times10^{48}$\,erg, nearly $10^4$ times the floor. The amplitude ratio
$\max|\Btor|/\max|\Bpol|$ falls to $1.19$: the poloidal field catches the
toroidal one in peak strength. In energy it only reaches $10\%$, because the
toroidal field occupies far more volume --- the two ratios saturate in different
places and quoting either alone misdescribes the state.

\paragraph{Decay, $t > 5.4$\,s.} $\Etor$ falls with an $e$-folding of
$3.44$\,s $= 1.44\,t_A$. At $t = 12$\,s, $4.9\%$ of the initial toroidal energy
remains.

\paragraph{The instability is $m=1$.} An azimuthal Fourier decomposition (the
\texttt{fmodes} tool, a direct projection over cells with no interpolation)
gives $|m{=}1|/|m{=}0| = 1.2\times10^{-16}$ at $t = 0$ --- the initial condition
is axisymmetric to machine precision. From $t \simeq 2.5$\,s \textbf{the poloidal
field becomes $m=1$ dominated}, with $|B_{z,m=1}|/|B_{z,m=0}|$ reaching 21 at
$t = 4$\,s, while the toroidal field stays largely axisymmetric ($m1/m0$ climbing
only to $0.14$). In the final state the toroidal $m=1$ saturates at $\simeq10\%$
of the axisymmetric component.

This rules out the axisymmetric Balbus--Hawley MRI, which in any case could not
exist on this grid: built from the \emph{vertical} field
($B_{\mathrm{pol,rms}} = 7.6\times10^8$\,G,
$v_{A,\mathrm{pol}} = 3.7\times10^4$\,cm\,s$^{-1}$), the most unstable wavelength
is $\lambda_{\mathrm{MRI}} = 2.9\times10^4$\,cm, or $0.003$ of a cell. What
remains is the Tayler kink or the non-axisymmetric toroidal-field MRI, both
$m\ge1$, which the azimuthal mode number alone cannot separate.

\subsection{Angular momentum}

$L_z$ falls at $0.206\%$\,s$^{-1}$ while the toroidal field lives and at
$0.099\%$\,s$^{-1}$ afterwards (Fig.~\ref{fig:long}b) --- \textbf{a factor of 2.1
slower once $95\%$ of the magnetic energy is gone}. This is direct evidence that
the angular momentum transport was magnetic. At the present rate, another $10\%$
of $L_z$ would take $\sim\!100$\,s.

\section{Numerical convergence}
\label{sec:conv}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\textwidth]{figures/convergence_192_256.pdf}
\caption{$192^3$ against $256^3$ over the growth phase. (a) poloidal energy with
the fits; (b) volume; (c) amplitude ratio with equipartition marked.}
\label{fig:conv}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/field_sequence_256.png}
\caption{$256^3$, same layout, planes and colour scales as
Fig.~\ref{fig:seq192}, at the corresponding instants.}
\label{fig:seq256}
\end{figure}

Three tests, three different verdicts.

\paragraph{Converged: mass and volume.} The mass lies in
$1.99971$--$2.00822\,\Msun$ at $192^3$ and $2.00208$--$2.00730$ at $256^3$; the
volume-equivalent radius differs by $0.03\%$ at $t=0$. There is no figure to
make.

\paragraph{Not converged: the growth rate.} This is the point that has to
accompany any version of the result:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
& $192^3$ & $256^3$ & ratio \\
\midrule
$\sigma$ from the poloidal energy & $1.369$ s$^{-1}$ & $2.007$ s$^{-1}$ & $1.47$ \\
$\sigma$ from the $m=1$ mode of $\Btor$ & $1.968$ s$^{-1}$ & $2.752$ s$^{-1}$ & $1.40$ \\
$\Epol(t=0)$, the numerical floor & $1.94\times10^{44}$ & $1.16\times10^{44}$ & $0.60$ \\
$\min(\max|\Btor|/\max|\Bpol|)$ & $1.19$ & $\mathbf{0.805}$ & \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The rate \emph{rises} by $47\%$ with resolution, measured by two independent
diagnostics that agree on the factor, and the finer grid starts from a
\emph{smaller} seed. It also carries the instability further: the poloidal field
overtakes the toroidal in peak strength at $256^3$ and never does at $192^3$.
\textbf{The measured $\sigma$ is a lower bound}, and so is the strength of the
instability.

\paragraph{Numerical: the early pulsation.} The first volume peak falls from
$2.51\,V_0$ to $1.79$ and the second from an amplitude of $0.49$ to $0.11$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:conv}b). The breathing that dominates the first few seconds is
largely the initial condition ringing on the grid. The persistent $1.50$\,s mode
that appears after $t=12$\,s is a different phenomenon and has not yet been
tested against resolution.

A note on cost: settling the rate would take $384^3$, about five times the cost
of $256^3$. The alternative --- and probably the right one --- is to introduce
explicit resistivity, giving the problem a physical dissipation scale instead of
letting the mesh set it. \textsc{Castro} does not currently implement it.

\section{Caveats}
\label{sec:caveats}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{The EOS is barotropic.} With \texttt{ztwd} there is no thermal
      reservoir: the $5.7\times10^{49}$\,erg that leave the field have no
      physical destination and are dissipated at the grid scale. We can state
      that the ordered field is destroyed; we cannot say where the energy went,
      nor how much heating it would cause in a real star. The stratification is
      also neutral, with no buoyancy to limit the mode --- in a stratified star
      the rate would be lower, which makes $\sigma$ a \emph{physical} upper bound
      at the same time as it is a \emph{numerical} lower bound.
\item \textbf{Part of the run lies outside the EOS validity range.} The peak
      field reaches $1.41\,B_c$ at $t = 4.0$\,s and exceeds the Landau critical
      field in $44\%$ of the sampled instants. \texttt{ztwd} assumes an
      unquantised gas.
\item \textbf{Tayler versus toroidal MRI is unresolved.} Both are $m\ge1$. The
      non-rotating case from the companion work does not decide it: there the
      star collapses radially in $2.5$\,s, through the axial column where
      $\Btor$ vanishes, and the non-axisymmetric mode is never reached. Set
      against that, the present result is that \textbf{rotation trades one fate
      for another}: the star stops collapsing and starts losing its field.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Status of the simulations}

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Extended $192^3$} (\texttt{wdrot192long}): running, already past
      $t = 22$\,s, target $60$\,s. The time step has quadrupled since $t=12$\,s
      as the field died, so it should finish well inside the nine windows
      budgeted.
\item \textbf{$256^3$} (\texttt{wdrot256}): running, $t \simeq 7.3$\,s of $12$.
      With CFL $0.3$ it crossed $t = 5.146$\,s, where two earlier attempts
      aborted deterministically.
\end{itemize}

code --- is under version control in the \texttt{WD\_MAG} repository.
