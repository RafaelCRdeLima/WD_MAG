Laura, your question is being answered right now. The non-rotating run is in
the queue as I write: the same star, the same K_tor, the same 1e9 G surface
dipole, with Omega_c = 0, evolved to the same 60 s as the rotating case. I
agree with your reading of why it matters -- rotation can stabilise what a
barotrope cannot, and if the ordered configuration we end up with survives
without it, that is a claim against the literature you mention rather than a
detail. I will send the answer when it finishes. Two things the equilibrium
already shows: without rotation the star weighs 1.407 Msun rather than 2.005,
so the extra mass in our production model is rotational, and it comes out
marginally prolate, R_pol/R_eq = 1.005, which is what Banibrata predicted.

Banibrata, your 0.01 turns out to be our number as well, arrived at from a
different direction. We ran a survey of some 1800 equilibria asking a purely
structural question: how much confined toroidal field can the star carry before
the magnetic pressure exceeds the gas pressure somewhere inside, so that the
field-free equation of state used to build the configuration stops describing
it? The answer is E_tor/|W| <= 0.0101, and it barely moves over a decade in
central density. It is converged: repeating the survey at 257^2 against 129^2
moves it by 0.01%.

Our production run then does what your bound would predict. It starts at
E_mag/|W| = 0.0114, marginally above, and the field decays until the ratio is
two orders of magnitude below, where it stops:

    t = 0 s     E_mag/|W| = 1.14e-2     E_tor/E_pol = 5e5
    t = 5 s                 3.9e-3                    7.0
    t = 12 s                1.4e-3                    4.2
    t = 45 s                1.2e-4                     77
    t = 78 s                2.8e-4                     89

So the run is better described as the star shedding field until it reaches a
configuration it can hold, rather than as the field being destroyed. I had put
it the wrong way round in August and the draft has been corrected accordingly.

Whether the two numbers coincide for a reason, we do not know. A virial
argument suggests they might: |W| is of order the volume integral of the
pressure, so E_mag/|W| is of order the volume average of 1/beta, and a global
energy bound and a local pressure floor would then be the same statement.

The other thing in the draft is the consequence of the ceiling for mass. A
field the star can hold is worth about +0.017 Msun, and the frontier it permits
converges to the Chandrasekhar mass from below without crossing it, ending at
neutronization. Adding differential rotation lifts the mass but not the
ceiling: we find configurations above 2 Msun with comparable poloidal and
toroidal energies, but their support is rotational and the field still carries
about one per cent of the virial.

Jorge, the correction I owe you from August is in the draft: the initial field
is not purely toroidal, there is a deliberate poloidal dipole, and on the grid
the poloidal component is some forty times larger than the model intends
because of discretisation. That matters for what seeds the m=1 mode, and it is
stated rather than buried.

One caveat I want to flag rather than have a referee find it. Our stability
criterion borrows Braithwaite's result, which is established for stably
stratified stars, and ours is barotropic. Laura's question is exactly the right
test of whether that borrowing is safe.
