This document explains what the dashboard computes: the equations behind
each number and each figure, and the code that implements them. It is not
an introduction to stellar physics or MHD --- it assumes the reader
already knows that. What this document does is bridge the theory
(section~\ref{sec:nucleo}) and the real code (\code{scf/}, \code{dashboard/}).

See \code{plano\_wd\_magnetizada.md} for the full project plan
(decisions D1--D6, architecture, phases). This document is about the
\emph{implemented physics}, not the work plan.

\textbf{Notation notes:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.3em]
\item The code uses the variable \code{omega} for $\varpi$ (cylindrical
  radius), \textbf{not} for angular velocity --- there is no rotation in
  this project (D3, static star). It is a naming choice specific to this
  code; whenever you read \code{omega} in \code{scf.py}/\code{gradshafranov.py},
  it always means $\varpi=r\sin\theta$.
\item $M$ is used in the source theory for two different objects: the
  total stellar mass and the Bernoulli potential $M(u)$
  (\S\ref{sec:bernoulli}). The code resolves the collision by naming the
  mass \code{M} (returned by \code{scf.total\_mass()}) and the potential
  \code{M\_u} (a local variable inside \code{hachisu\_scf()}, never
  exposed outside the function). In this document, whenever there is risk
  of ambiguity, the text spells out ``mass'' or ``Bernoulli potential''
  in full.
\item \code{Bth} appears as \code{Btheta} in \code{diagnostics.py}
  (function parameter) and \code{Bth} on the dashboard pages (local
  variable) --- same object, two names.
\end{itemize}

\section{Common theoretical core}
\label{sec:nucleo}

\subsection{Equation of state}
\label{sec:eos}

Fully degenerate electron gas at $T=0$:
\begin{align}
P &= A\left[x(2x^2-3)(x^2+1)^{1/2} + 3\sinh^{-1}x\right]\\
\rho &= Bx^3\\
x &\equiv \frac{p_F}{m_ec}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
A &= 6.01\times10^{22}\ \mathrm{dyn\,cm^{-2}}\\
B &= \frac{9.82\times10^{5}}{Y_e}\ \mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}} \qquad (Y_e=0.5 \Rightarrow B\approx1.96\times10^6)
\end{align}

$A$ is a fixed physical constant (a combination of $m_e$, $c$, $\hbar$);
$B$ depends on composition only through $Y_e$ (electrons per baryon). The
dashboard parametrizes by $\mu_e=1/Y_e$ instead of $Y_e$ directly.

\arrowto{scf/eos.py :: pressure()} (P(x)), \code{density()} ($\rho(x)$), \code{B\_of\_mu\_e()} ($B(\mu_e)$)

Specific enthalpy, analytic --- this is what makes the SCF possible:
\begin{equation}
H = \int \frac{dP}{\rho} = \frac{8A}{B}\left[\sqrt{1+x^2}-1\right]
\end{equation}

\arrowto{scf/eos.py :: enthalpy()}

Inverse, applied at every SCF iteration:
\begin{equation}
x = \sqrt{\left(1+\frac{HB}{8A}\right)^2 - 1} \qquad (H\le0 \Rightarrow \rho=0)
\end{equation}

\arrowto{scf/eos.py :: x\_of\_enthalpy(), density\_of\_enthalpy()}

The $-1$ term normalizes $H=0$ at the surface (the $\rho=0$ boundary of the star).

\textbf{Effective polytropic index.} Near the surface $x\ll1$ gives
$\rho\propto H^{3/2}$, i.e. $n=3/2$. In the relativistic core $x\gg1$
gives $\rho\propto H^3$, i.e. $n=3$. The EOS slides between the two, and
$n=3$ is the marginal case where the mass becomes independent of the
central density --- the origin of the Chandrasekhar limit. This fact is
the direct cause of the parametrization decision in
\S\ref{sec:gs}/\S\ref{sec:bernoulli}.

\textbf{Sound speed.} Added after the original theoretical spine (it was
not in the plan), because it is needed for the amplitude of the
perturbation exported in Tab 3:
\begin{equation}
c_s = \sqrt{\frac{dP}{d\rho}}\ , \qquad \frac{dP}{dx} = \frac{8Ax^4}{\sqrt{1+x^2}} \quad \text{(analytic derivative of $P(x)$)}
\end{equation}

\arrowto{scf/eos.py :: sound\_speed()}, validated against a finite difference in \code{scf/tests/test\_sound\_speed.py}

\subsection{Self-gravity}

\begin{equation}
\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho
\end{equation}

Solved by expansion in Legendre polynomials. With
$\rho(r,\theta) = \sum_l \rho_l(r)P_l(\cos\theta)$:
\begin{equation}
\Phi_l(r) = -\frac{4\pi G}{2l+1}\left[
  r^{-(l+1)}\int_0^r \rho_l(r')\,r'^{\,l+2}\,dr'
  \ +\ r^{l}\int_r^\infty \rho_l(r')\,r'^{\,1-l}\,dr'
\right]
\end{equation}

The weights $r'^{\,l+2}$ and $r'^{\,1-l}$ carry the $r'^2$ of the volume
element.

\arrowto{scf/poisson.py :: solve\_poisson()} (exact implementation of this formula, $D_l/E_l$ computed by cumulative trapezoidal sums); \code{legendre\_matrix()} computes $P_l(\cos\theta)$.

Validated against the closed-form solution of the uniform sphere and
Newton's shell theorem in \code{scf/tests/test\_poisson.py}.

$G=6.674\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{cm^3\,g^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ --- a fixed physical
constant (rounded CODATA reference value), not a project parameter.
\arrowto{scf/poisson.py :: G\_CONST}, repeated in \code{dashboard/units.py :: G\_CONST} (same value, two modules, because \code{poisson.py} cannot depend on the dashboard --- R1).

\subsection{Magnetic field and the flux function}

With $\varpi=r\sin\theta$, the flux function is defined as
\begin{equation}
u = \varpi A_\varphi
\end{equation}
and the axisymmetric field splits into poloidal plus toroidal:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{B} = \frac{1}{\varpi}\left[\nabla u \times \hat{e}_\varphi \ +\ \beta(u)\,\hat{e}_\varphi\right]
\end{equation}
In spherical components:
\begin{align}
B_r &= \frac{1}{r^2\sin\theta}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}\\
B_\theta &= -\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\\
B_\varphi &= \frac{\beta(u)}{\varpi}
\end{align}

\arrowto{scf/diagnostics.py :: poloidal\_field()} implements $B_r$, $B_\theta$ (via finite differences of $u$, protected against the coordinate singularity at $\varpi\to0$). $B_\varphi$ is not computed from an intermediate $\beta(u)$; it comes directly out of \code{scf/toroidal.py :: impose\_toroidal()}, which already implements the chosen functional form for $\beta$ (\S\ref{sec:toroidal}) substituted into the formula above.

The general form $\mathbf{B}=\frac{1}{\varpi}[\nabla u\times\hat e_\varphi+\beta\hat e_\varphi]$ does not appear anywhere in the code as a vector identity --- the code goes straight to the three scalar components.

\textbf{Geometric interpretation: the contours of $u$ are the poloidal
field lines.} That's why Tab 1 plots them (\S\ref{sec:aba1}).

\subsection{Grad-Shafranov}
\label{sec:gs}

\begin{align}
\Delta^* u &= -4\pi\varpi^2\rho f(u) - \beta\beta'(u)\\
\Delta^* &= \frac{\partial^2}{\partial\varpi^2} - \frac{1}{\varpi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varpi} + \frac{\partial^2}{\partial z^2}
\end{align}

$f(u)=dM/du$ is the current function generating the poloidal field;
$\beta(u)$ generates the toroidal field. The simplest choice, and the one
used here, is $f(u)=k_0$ constant (Lander \& Jones 2009). Since the
toroidal field is imposed after the poloidal field has converged
(\S\ref{sec:toroidal}, D6) and does not enter the GS equation actually
solved by the SCF, the term $-\beta\beta'(u)$ \textbf{does not appear in
the source the code actually assembles} --- the implemented source is
only $-4\pi\varpi^2\rho k_0$.

\arrowto{scf/gradshafranov.py :: solve\_gradshafranov()} --- source passed by \code{scf/scf.py :: hachisu\_scf()} as \code{source = -4*np.pi*omega2*rho*k0}.

The current density corresponding to this source is
\begin{equation}
J_\varphi = c\rho\varpi f(u)
\end{equation}

This formula \textbf{does not correspond to any function in the code} ---
$J_\varphi$ is never computed as a named quantity. It was used only
analytically, during the debugging that found the bug described in the
box below, to assemble the right-hand side of the integral Ampère's-law
test (derivative-free tests, further below).

\textbf{Solution by associated-Legendre expansion.} The operator
$\Delta^*$ separates into $P_l^1(\cos\theta)$ functions, \textbf{not}
$P_l(\cos\theta)$. The radial structure of the Green's function is
analogous to that of Poisson, but the integral weights \textbf{are not
the same}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
& inner weight & outer weight\\
\midrule
Poisson (for $\Phi$) & $r'^{\,l+2}$ & $r'^{\,1-l}$\\
Grad-Shafranov (for $u$) & $r'^{\,l+1}$ & $r'^{\,-l}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\arrowto{scf/gradshafranov.py :: solve\_gradshafranov()}

In the lines where \code{D\_l}/\code{E\_l} are assembled with \code{r ** (l + 1)} and \code{r ** (-l)}.

\begin{whybox}{Why it's like this --- the Green's-function exponents}
The difference of one power comes from the $\varpi$ weight of the GS
source compared to the $r'^2$ of the volume element in Poisson, and
depends on whether the equation is solved for $u$ or for $A_\varphi$.
\textbf{This was a real bug in this project:} an earlier version of
\code{gradshafranov.py} used $r'^{\,l+2}$ and $r'^{\,1-l}$ (mistakenly
copied from the structure of \code{poisson.py}, which has one extra
power of $r$ because of the substitution $\chi=r\Phi_l$ used in reducing
the scalar Laplacian --- $\Delta^*$ does not need that substitution). The
bug inflated the poloidal field by a factor of $\sim7000\times$ and the
magnetic energy by $\sim5\times10^7$.

The bug survived piece-by-piece validation --- including a ``by-hand''
closed form --- because that closed form was built by reusing the same
indicial equation ($r^{l+1}$, $r^{-l}$ for the homogeneous solutions,
which \textbf{were correct}) and therefore inherited the same
normalization of the internal integrals, which was wrong. Only two tests
that use neither the Green's function nor a second derivative ---
consistency via Ampère's law --- revealed the fixed factor. See the full
note (with the reasoning chain) at the top of \code{scf/gradshafranov.py}.

\smallskip
\textbf{Operational lesson:} ``solve with the formula, check against the
same formula'' is not independent validation, even across different
files/functions, if both inherit the same normalization from a shared
derivation.
\end{whybox}

\textbf{Derivative-free normalization tests} --- the defense against this
class of error:
\begin{align}
\text{Flux:}\quad & u(\varpi,z) = \int_0^\varpi B_z(\varpi',z)\,\varpi'\,d\varpi'\\
\text{Ampère:}\quad & \oint_C \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 4\pi k_0\int_S \rho\varpi\,dA
\end{align}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Ampère}: implemented and in the repository
  \arrowto{scf/tests/test\_gradshafranov.py :: test\_ampere\_law()}
  Uses a synthetic case with a pure $l=1$-mode source (not the full
  EOS/SCF), computes $\oint\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$ on a rectangular
  loop in $(r,\theta)$ and compares it against
  $-\iint[\mathrm{source}/\sin\theta]\,dr\,d\theta$ (an equivalent form
  derived from Ampère's law for this geometry). Closes to $\sim2\%$.
\item \textbf{Flux}: \emph{proposed} during debugging (see the
  conversation history that fixed the bug) but \textbf{not implemented as
  a separate test} --- the Ampère test alone already revealed and
  confirmed the bug fix, so the flux consistency test was never actually
  written. Recorded as a gap in \S\ref{sec:aberto}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Bernoulli and the parametrization}
\label{sec:bernoulli}

\begin{equation}
H + \Phi - M(u) = C\ , \qquad M(u) = \int f(u)\,du
\end{equation}

With $f=k_0$ constant, $M(u)=k_0u$.

The integration constant is fixed \textbf{at the center}:
\begin{equation}
C = H(\rho_c) + \Phi_c - M(u_c)
\end{equation}

where $\Phi_c$, $u_c$ here denote the values at the center ($r=0$) ---
\textbf{note}: this use of $u_c$ (value of $u$ at the center) is
different from the $u_c$ used in \S\ref{sec:toroidal} (value of $u$ on
the last closed line, at the surface). They are the same symbol for two
different evaluation points; the code never needs both at once, but the
reader should note the collision. Since $\varpi=r\sin\theta$ vanishes at
the center for any $\theta$, $u_c(\text{center})=0$ always and the term
$M(u_c)$ disappears --- it stays in the formula for generality, not
because it contributes.

\arrowto{scf/scf.py :: hachisu\_scf()} --- line \code{C = H\_c + Phi[0, 0] - M\_u[0, 0]}.

\begin{whybox}{Why it's like this --- the $(\rho_c, k_0)$ parametrization}
The classical Hachisu recipe fixes $C$ by imposing $H=0$ at two surface
points (pole and equator). This is ill-posed for this EOS: since $n\to3$
in the core (\S\ref{sec:eos}), the mass becomes nearly independent of the
central density and the radius plunges, so solving for $C$ from the
radius inverts a nearly-singular map. Tested and confirmed in this
project: the Picard iteration under that recipe is linearly unstable
across the whole range $\rho_c=10^6$--$10^{10}$ g/cm$^3$, with or without
sub-relaxation.

The adopted parametrization is by \textbf{$(\rho_c, k_0)$}, two
independent inputs, with no surface condition --- $C$ is fixed by a local
condition ($H$ at the center), not a global one (an integral over the
whole mass). This change alone solved the instability: geometric
convergence to machine precision in $\sim$12 iterations, versus
exponential divergence of the old recipe.

\smallskip
\textbf{Caveat:} in the strong-field regime, when the density peak
migrates away from the center, this anchor also loses the physical
meaning that makes it stable --- see \S\ref{sec:limitacoes}.
\end{whybox}

\subsection{The SCF loop}
\label{sec:loop}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.6em,itemsep=0.15em,label=\arabic*.]
\item $\rho \leftarrow$ initial guess (spherical $n=3$ polytrope)
\item $A_\varphi \leftarrow 0$
\item \textbf{repeat:}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.6em,itemsep=0.15em,label=\arabic*.,start=4]
\item $\Phi \leftarrow \mathrm{Poisson}(\rho)$
\item $A_\varphi \leftarrow \mathrm{GradShafranov}(\rho, f)$
\item $u \leftarrow \varpi A_\varphi$\ ;\ $M \leftarrow \int f\,du$
\item $C \leftarrow H(\rho_c) + \Phi_c - M(u_c)$
\item $H \leftarrow C - \Phi + M(u)$
\item $\rho_{new} \leftarrow \mathrm{EOS}^{-1}(H)$ \qquad $[H\le0 \Rightarrow \rho=0]$
\item $\rho \leftarrow (1-\omega)\rho + \omega\rho_{new}$ \qquad $\omega\approx0.3$
\end{enumerate}
\item[11.] \textbf{until} $\max|\Delta\rho|/\rho_c < \mathrm{tol}$
\end{enumerate}

\arrowto{scf/scf.py :: hachisu\_scf()} implements steps 1, 3--9 and 11 literally (step 2 is implicit: $u$ starts at zero before the first iteration).

\textbf{Discrepancy with the real code, step 10:} the implementation
\textbf{does not do sub-relaxation}. The corresponding line in
\code{hachisu\_scf()} is \code{rho = rho\_new} --- a direct replacement,
equivalent to $\omega=1$, not $\omega\approx0.3$. There is no $\omega$
parameter in the function signature. This is not an oversight: it is a
direct consequence of the change described in the \S\ref{sec:bernoulli}
box. The old recipe (two surface conditions) required sub-relaxation to
try to stabilize an iteration that was unstable regardless; the
$(\rho_c,k_0)$ parametrization converges by direct replacement because
the instability that sub-relaxation was trying to mask was removed at
the root. See the project note at the top of \code{scf/scf.py} for the
full history (including tests showing that sub-relaxing the old recipe
did not fix the problem).

The convergence criterion (\code{tol}) is a numerical, not physical,
parameter --- the dashboard exposes values from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-8}$
(Tab 1), with $10^{-6}$ as the default.

\subsection{Virial and energy diagnostics}
\label{sec:virial}

\begin{align}
W &= \tfrac{1}{2}\int \rho\Phi\,dV & &\text{(gravitational, negative)}\\
\Pi &= \int P\,dV & &\text{(internal)}\\
E_{pol} &= \int \frac{B_r^2+B_\theta^2}{8\pi}\,dV\\
E_{tor} &= \int \frac{B_\varphi^2}{8\pi}\,dV\\
\mathcal{M} &= E_{pol} + E_{tor}
\end{align}

\arrowto{scf/diagnostics.py :: gravitational\_energy()} (W), \code{pressure\_integral()} ($\Pi$), \code{magnetic\_energies()} ($E_{pol}$, $E_{tor}$, $\mathcal{M}$ --- called \code{E\_mag} in the code). All use \code{volume\_integral()} as their base ($dV=r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\varphi$, $\varphi$ integrated analytically to $2\pi$).

Scalar virial theorem for a static configuration:
\begin{equation}
W + 3\Pi + \mathcal{M} = 0
\end{equation}

Virial error, used as a quality gate:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{VE} = \frac{|W+3\Pi+\mathcal{M}|}{|W|} \qquad \text{acceptance criterion: } \mathrm{VE}<10^{-3}
\end{equation}

\arrowto{scf/diagnostics.py :: virial\_error()} The $10^{-3}$ threshold is a project convention (V3 of the plan), not a physical constant --- it appears hard-coded as a comparison in \code{dashboard/pages/1\_equilibrium.py} and \code{3\_export.py}, not in \code{units.py} nor in \code{diagnostics.py} (no physics module defines this threshold as a named constant --- it is a UI/gate decision repeated in two pages).

\textbf{Magnetic virial identity.} Exact, and it is the test that links
the magnetic sector to the gravitational one:
\begin{equation}
\int \rho\,\nabla M(u)\cdot\mathbf{r}\,dV \ =\ \int \frac{B^2}{8\pi}\,dV
\end{equation}

This identity \textbf{does not correspond to any function in the code}.
It was verified numerically in an ad hoc way during the debugging of the
\S\ref{sec:gs} bug (comparing $k_0\int\rho\,r\,\partial u/\partial r\,dV$
with $\int B^2/8\pi\,dV$ for a converged solution), but there is no
function in \code{scf/diagnostics.py} that computes it nor a committed
test that exercises it. Recorded in \S\ref{sec:aberto} as a gap.

\begin{notebox}{Mandatory conceptual note}
$M(u)$ \textbf{is not} the magnetic energy. It is the specific potential
of the Lorentz force --- only the part that enters the Bernoulli
equation. There is no \emph{local} identity between $M(u)$ and
$B^2/8\pi$. The \emph{global} identity above is what forces the two
quantities to agree in order of magnitude. Measured in this project, in
the linear regime (perturbative field), the ratio
$(E_{mag}/|W|)/(M_u/H_c)$ is $\approx0.5$ and is constant in $k_0$
(confirmed by doubling $k_0$: both ratios quadruple, the ratio between
them does not change); it drifts to $\sim0.38$ as the field stops being
a small perturbation ($k_0\gtrsim10^{-12}$ in the $\rho_c=10^9$,
$R\approx3\times10^8$ cm configuration). This was the observation that,
together with the Ampère test, confirmed that the bug in the
\S\ref{sec:gs} box was real and not a units artifact.
\end{notebox}

\textbf{Physical limit.} $\mathcal{M}<|W|$ is a rigid constraint:
$E_{mag}/|W|\ge1$ is an impossible configuration (it would violate the
virial with $\Pi\ge0$). Use it as a sanity anchor when reading any
result --- if the dashboard shows $E_{mag}/|W|$ above a few tenths, be
suspicious before believing it.

\left(\frac{B_t}{B_p}\right)_{\text{amplitude}} &= \frac{\max|B_\varphi|}{\max|B_{pol}|}
\end{align}

They differ by orders of magnitude because the toroidal field is confined

\subsection{The toroidal field and the twisted torus}
\label{sec:toroidal}

In the barotropic formulation, $\beta=\beta(u)$: the toroidal function
depends only on the flux function. It is this condition that cancels the
$\varphi$ component of the Lorentz force and allows the Bernoulli
integral to exist (\S\ref{sec:bernoulli}).

Geometric consequence: outside the star there is no current, so
$B_\varphi=0$ there; since $\beta=\beta(u)$, it must vanish on every flux
line that escapes the surface. \textbf{The toroidal field is
automatically confined to the region of closed poloidal lines} --- the
twisted torus is not imposed by geometric decree, it falls out of the
consistency of the equation.

Adopted functional form:
\begin{equation}
\beta(u) = \zeta\,(u-u_c)^{m+1}\,\Theta(u-u_c)\ , \qquad m\ge1
\end{equation}

with $u_c$ the value of $u$ on the last closed line (here, $u_c$ = the
value of $u$ at the surface --- see the notation distinction in
\S\ref{sec:bernoulli}). The exponent $\ge1$ keeps $\beta\beta'$
continuous at the edge of the torus.

\arrowto{scf/toroidal.py :: impose\_toroidal()} implements this form already substituted into $B_\varphi=\beta/\varpi$: $B_\varphi=\zeta(u-u_c)^{m+1}/\varpi$ for $u>u_c$, $0$ outside. \code{find\_uc()} implements the search for $u_c$ as the \textbf{maximum of $u$ along the entire stellar surface} ($H=0$, sweeping $\theta$ from pole to equator) --- it is this specific choice of ``last closed line'' that the code uses; contours with $u$ larger than that maximum, by definition, do not touch the surface anywhere and remain entirely interior.

$\zeta$ is not a direct user input parameter --- the dashboard asks for
the target $B_t/B_p$ (energy) ratio and solves for $\zeta$ to reach it,
exploiting the fact that $B_\varphi$ is linear in $\zeta$ (so $E_{tor}$
is quadratic):

\arrowto{scf/toroidal.py :: solve\_zeta\_for\_energy\_ratio()}

\begin{whybox}{Why the toroidal field is imposed rather than solved for}
The condition $\beta=\beta(u)$ confines the toroidal field to a
small-volume torus, and the resulting energy ratio --- if $\beta$ were
extracted from a general barotropic closure condition instead of chosen
freely --- comes out to a few percent. \textbf{It is not possible to
reach $B_t/B_p\sim1/2$ that way.} That's why the project solves the
barotropic SCF only for the poloidal field (\S\ref{sec:gs}--\S\ref{sec:loop},
with $f(u)=k_0$) and imposes the toroidal field on top, at the desired
ratio ($\zeta$ solved for the target), loading it into Castro out of
exact equilibrium and relaxing it with damping (sponge, Tab 3). For a
dynamical study, exact equilibrium is not required --- it just needs to
be close enough that the transient doesn't destroy the topology.
\end{whybox}

\arrowto{scf/toroidal.py :: closed\_torus\_volume\_fraction()}

\textbf{Torus radial extent} (used to check mesh resolution, Tab 3):
\arrowto{scf/toroidal.py :: torus\_radial\_extent()}, measured along the equator by default.

\subsection{Rotation and the self-consistent toroidal branch}
\label{sec:rotation}

Extension added after the original poloidal-only spine, motivated by the
collaboration's interest in $\sim2\,M_\odot$ white dwarfs as SN~Ia
progenitors: differential rotation plus a toroidal field, because the two
deformations oppose each other (rotation flattens, toroidal elongates),
which pushes back the mass-shedding limit.

\textbf{Architecture.} \code{scf.hachisu\_scf()} takes three optional
\emph{terms} (\code{scf/terms/}) instead of one function per physics
combination --- each term contributes an additive piece to the same
Bernoulli equation:
\begin{equation}
H + \Phi - C_{rot}(\varpi) - M_{pol}(u) + M_{tor}(\rho\varpi^2) = C
\end{equation}
rearranged for the loop: $H = C - \Phi + C_{rot}(\varpi) + M_{pol}(u) -
M_{tor}(\rho\varpi^2)$. \code{rotation}/\code{poloidal}/\code{toroidal} =
\code{None} turns a term off (zero contribution, zero energy).
\code{poloidal} and \code{toroidal} are mutually exclusive (out of scope:
self-consistent mixed field --- same reason as D6, barotropy does not
deliver a free Bt/Bp). With all three \code{None}, the loop reproduces
the pre-extension code \textbf{bit for bit} (checked against a frozen
copy of the old \code{scf.py}, \code{scf/tests/test\_regression\_v0.py}).

\textbf{Rotation} (\code{scf/terms/rotation.py}): j-constant law,
\begin{equation}
\Omega(\varpi) = \frac{\Omega_c A^2}{A^2+\varpi^2}
\end{equation}
with rigid rotation as the $A\to\infty$ limit, handled analytically (not
a large-but-finite $A$ substitute). Its Bernoulli potential has a closed
form,
\begin{equation}
C_{rot}(\varpi) = \frac{\Omega_c^2 A^2}{2}\frac{\varpi^2}{A^2+\varpi^2}
\end{equation}
reducing to $\tfrac{1}{2}\Omega_c^2\varpi^2$ as $A\to\infty$.

The virial theorem gains the rotational kinetic term:
\begin{align}
2T + W + 3\Pi + \mathcal{M} &= 0 \ ,\qquad T = \tfrac{1}{2}\int \rho\,\Omega^2(\varpi)\,\varpi^2\,dV\\
\mathrm{VE} &= \frac{|2T+W+3\Pi+\mathcal{M}|}{|W|}
\end{align}

\arrowto{diagnostics.virial\_error()} (now takes \code{T=0.0}, default reduces exactly to the old formula) and \code{diagnostics.virial\_error\_terms()} (the term-aware wrapper --- pulls $T$/$B_r$/$B_\theta$/$B_\varphi$ from whichever terms are active and calls \code{virial\_error()}, so the residual formula itself lives in exactly one place).

\textbf{Stability/termination diagnostics:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item $T/|W|$: $\ge0.14$ secular non-axisymmetric instability threshold
  (Ostriker \& Bodenheimer 1973), $\ge0.27$ dynamical bar-mode threshold.
  Same traffic-light convention as VE; blocks export (Tab 3) at
  $\ge0.14$, same as VE's R5.
\item Equatorial mass-loss ratio: $\Omega^2(R_{eq})R_{eq} \big/ (d\Phi/dr$
  at $R_{eq}$, equator$)$ --- $\to1$ signals the effective gravity
  vanishing at the equator (Keplerian breakup).
  \arrowto{diagnostics.equatorial\_mass\_loss\_ratio()}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Self-consistent purely toroidal field}
(\code{scf/terms/toroidal\_sc.py}): $B_\varphi = K\rho^m\varpi^{2m-1}$,
$m\ge1$. Derived from the Lorentz force (Maxwell stress tensor, verified
symbolically with SymPy for $m=1,2,3/2$ --- residual exactly 0;
\code{scf/tests/test\_toroidal\_sc.py}):
\begin{equation}
M_{tor}(s) = \frac{mK^2}{4\pi(2m-1)}\,s^{2m-1}\ ,\qquad s=\rho\varpi^2
\end{equation}

Unlike $M_{pol}(u)$, $M_{tor}$ depends on $\rho$ --- the \emph{unknown}
being solved for at each grid point --- so step 9 of the SCF loop
(\S\ref{sec:loop}) stops being a direct EOS inversion and becomes a
per-point root find ($H(\rho)+M_{tor}(\rho\varpi^2)=\mathrm{RHS}$,
monotonic increasing in $\rho$, safe to bracket with
\code{scipy.optimize.brentq} --- \arrowto{scf.py :: \_solve\_rho\_implicit()}). This branch does \textbf{not} use Grad-Shafranov at all --- no flux function, no $\Delta^*$, no Green's function; $B_\varphi$ is an algebraic function of the local $\rho$, not a PDE solution.

Validated: turning on the field at fixed $\rho_c$ increases the mass
(sign check); pure toroidal field produces \textbf{prolate} deformation
($R_{pol}>R_{eq}$, opposite of the poloidal/oblate case), VE closes
($\sim4\times10^{-4}$).

\begin{notebox}{Correction --- the sign of the magnetic virial identity, and why it is not ``$M_{tor}$ depends on $\rho$''}
The magnetic virial identity for this branch is
\begin{equation}
\int \rho\,\nabla M_{tor}\cdot\mathbf{r}\,dV \ =\ -\int \frac{B_\varphi^2}{8\pi}\,dV
\end{equation}
a \textbf{minus} sign --- confirmed by an independent derivation (Maxwell
stress tensor divergence: $\int\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{f}_L\,dV=+\int
B^2/8\pi\,dV$ in general, for any purely toroidal field, regardless of
any Bernoulli convention) and by clean numerical convergence with grid
refinement ($2.27\%\to1.00\%\to0.36\%\to0.14\%$ at $nr=65\to257$).

The first version of this note attributed the sign to ``$M_{tor}$ depends
on $\rho$, $M_{pol}$ does not.'' \textbf{That attribution was wrong},
caught on review. Here is the actual mechanism: writing the master
Bernoulli as $H+\Phi-C_{rot}-M_{pol}+M_{tor}=C$ (note the signs: $M_{pol}$
enters with a minus, $M_{tor}$ with a plus) and comparing its gradient
against the momentum equation gives $\mathbf{F}_{L,tor}/\rho=
-\nabla M_{tor}$ --- the minus comes directly from $M_{tor}$ having been
written with a \textbf{plus} sign in the master equation, full stop.
Substituting into the sign-fixed, convention-free Maxwell identity
$\int\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{F}_L\,dV=+\int B^2/8\pi\,dV$ is what produces
the minus sign in the virial identity. Had the master equation instead
been written with $-M_{tor}$, the virial identity would come out $+$,
with $M_{tor}$ depending on $\rho$ in exactly the same way either time.
\textbf{$\rho$ entering $M_{tor}$ is why the SCF inversion becomes
implicit (a fact about the algorithm's structure) --- it has nothing to
do with which sign the virial identity carries (a fact about which
convention was chosen when the term was first written into the Bernoulli
equation).} Two different questions, easy to conflate, and conflating
them is exactly the kind of mistake this project has already been burned
by once (\S\ref{sec:gs}'s Green's-function bug survived because
independent-looking checks secretly shared one derivation). Get the
causal story right here or a future reader ``fixing'' the (correct) code
to match the (wrong) causal explanation is a real, specific risk --- see
\code{scf/terms/toroidal\_sc.py} for the full derivation.
\end{notebox}

\textbf{V-R1/V-R2 status (rigid vs.\ differential rotation, no field).}
V-R2 (differential, j-constant, $A=0.3\,R_{guess}$) reaches
$2.19\,M_\odot$ against the $\sim2.2\,M_\odot$ target (Yoon \& Langer
2005) --- \textbf{0.40\% error}, reached with
$\mathrm{mass\_loss\_ratio}\sim0.13$, comfortably away from breakup.
\code{scf/tests/test\_differential\_rotation.py}.

V-R1 (rigid) \textbf{does not validate} against the $\sim1.5\,M_\odot$
target (Hachisu et al.\ 2012; Boshkayev et al.\ 2013). At
$\rho_c=10^{12}$, stepping $\Omega_c$ upward with continuation, the
sequence terminates \emph{numerically} at $R_{pol}/R_{eq}\approx0.932$,
$\Omega_c\approx26.47$, with \textbf{mass\_loss\_ratio only 0.135} ---
far from the $\to1$ that a genuine mass-shedding termination requires,
and far from the Roche-model expectation for a centrally-condensed
($n\approx3$) configuration, $R_{pol}/R_{eq}=2/3\approx0.667$ at real
breakup. The cause is not resolved. Two suspects, not yet investigated
(not on the project's critical path --- Jorge wants differential
rotation, which already works): outer-layer radial resolution, and ---
most likely --- $\Omega_c$ itself being a bad control parameter this
close to the sequence's end (a documented phenomenon in the
rotating-SCF literature; Hachisu's own method sidesteps it by
parametrizing on axis ratio and solving for $\Omega^2$, not the
reverse). A direct test confirms this: bisecting for a target axis ratio
instead of imposing $\Omega_c$ directly saturates at the same point ---
no smaller axis ratio is reachable by continuation in $\Omega_c$, for
any target. The mechanism itself is not in doubt: $(M-M_0)/M_0$ tracks
$T/|W|$ with a stable proportionality coefficient ($\sim3.0$) across the
whole tested range, and V-R6 (below) independently confirms $T$. See
\code{scf/tests/test\_rotation.py} for the full numbers and reasoning
chain.

\begin{whybox}{Bug found and fixed during this investigation (unrelated to the V-R1 question above, but real and previously unnoticed)}
\code{diagnostics.surface\_radius()} (and everything built on it ---
\code{equatorial\_polar\_radii()}, \code{surface\_field()},
\code{surface\_dipolarity()}, \code{toroidal.find\_uc()},
\code{impose\_toroidal()}) used to take $\rho$ and linear-interpolate
where it crosses zero. But $\rho=\mathrm{EOS}^{-1}(H)$ is clipped to
\emph{exactly} $0.0$ for $H\le0$ (\code{eos.density\_of\_enthalpy}) ---
so the grid point just past the surface always has $\rho=0.0$ exactly,
which makes the interpolation fraction collapse to exactly $1.0$ and the
function always return a raw grid point, never a true sub-grid position.
Radii were silently grid-quantized project-wide. Fixed by interpolating
on $H$ instead (continuous, crosses zero smoothly between grid points,
never clipped) --- verified to shift $R_{eq}$/$R_{pol}$ by tens of km on
a modest mesh ($nr=65$), not a cosmetic correction. Found by noticing
that a rotation sequence's $R_{pol}/R_{eq}$ was landing on suspiciously
exact small integer ratios ($37/39$, $38/39$, \ldots) --- the classic
signature of grid quantization, not physics.
\end{whybox}

\textbf{T (Fase 1, V-R6).} Computed two independent ways --- once via
\code{rotation.energy()}, once via a from-scratch volume integral
written in the test, not calling any shared code beyond $\Omega(\varpi)$
--- and the two agree to machine precision ($\mathrm{rel\_diff}=0.00\times10^{0}$).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Validity gate: $\rho_c$ and inverse beta decay}{Validity gate: rho_c and inverse beta decay}}
\label{sec:rho-c-gate}

$\rho_c$ was, until now, the only input with no validity gate --- VE and
$T/|W|$ already have traffic lights, $\rho_c$ did not, and the slider
went up to $10^{12}$ with no warning even though matter neutronizes long
before that density: past the inverse-beta-decay (electron capture)
threshold, the constant-$\mu_e$ cold EOS used throughout this project
(\S\ref{sec:eos}) stops describing a real white dwarf --- composition
changes locally, and the resulting configuration (radius of order tens
of km at these densities) is not a white dwarf in any recognizable sense
(see the Das \& Mukhopadhyay 2.58~$M_\odot$/70~km example in
\code{plano\_wd\_magnetizada.md}).

Threshold densities, from \textbf{Table 2 of Boshkayev, Rueda, Ruffini \&
Siutsou (2013), ApJ 762, 117} (threshold energies from Audi et al.\ 2003,
critical density via the RFMT EOS --- verified against the published
table, not re-derived here):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Nuclide & $\mu_e = A/Z$ & $\rho_{threshold}$ (g/cm$^3$)\\
\midrule
$^4$He & 2.0 & $1.39\times10^{11}$\\
$^{12}$C & 2.0 & $3.97\times10^{10}$\\
$^{16}$O & 2.0 & $1.94\times10^{10}$\\
$^{56}$Fe & $56/26\approx2.154$ & $1.18\times10^{9}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

$\mu_e$ does not uniquely fix composition --- $^4$He, $^{12}$C and

capture threshold depends on the specific nuclide's binding energy, not
$\mu_e$ alone. This project has no composition selector beyond $\mu_e$,

better to warn too early than too late --- and linearly interpolates
toward the $^{56}$Fe point for $\mu_e$ up to $56/26$; outside
$[2.0,\,2.154]$ it returns the nearest tabulated value (flat
extrapolation, no invented trend).

\textbf{Practical consequence:} the dashboard's own default
($\rho_c=10^{12}$, chosen so the field-free case reproduces the
Chandrasekhar limit within 1\%, V1) is itself \textbf{above} the
$^{16}$O threshold ($1.94\times10^{10}$) --- that default was always an
asymptotic numerical convenience (the $M(\rho_c)$ curve for the
idealized $T=0$ free-electron-gas EOS saturates toward $1.44\,M_\odot$
only as $\rho_c\to\infty$, ignoring neutronization), never a claim that
$\rho_c=10^{12}$ is a physically realizable white dwarf.
\textbf{Physically valid $\rho_c$ for this EOS, standard C/O or He
composition, is $\lesssim2\times10^{10}$ g/cm$^3$} ($^{16}$O; up to
$\sim1.4\times10^{11}$ for pure $^4$He) --- V1's high-$\rho_c$ sequence
is a numerical asymptote check, not a claim about real stars, and any
run intended for Castro export needs $\rho_c$ inside the physical range.

\section{Tab 2 --- Sweep}

\arrowto{dashboard/pages/2\_sweep.py, dashboard/sweep\_worker.py}

Runs the SCF (\S\ref{sec:loop}) on a $(\rho_c,k_0)$ grid in parallel
(\code{ProcessPoolExecutor}), with caching by parameter hash
(\code{store.run\_exists()}/\code{store.save\_run()}, \S\ref{sec:aba4}).
Each grid point is an independent call to \code{scf.hachisu\_scf()}
followed by the same diagnostics as Tab 1 --- no new physics, just
orchestration.

\arrowto{dashboard/sweep\_worker.py :: run\_one()} --- the picklable function each grid worker process runs.

\textbf{What an equilibrium sequence is.} Fixing $\rho_c$ and varying
$k_0$ (or vice versa) produces a sequence of equilibrium configurations.
\textbf{The sequence ends} when the SCF stops converging or when VE
exceeds the acceptance threshold (\S\ref{sec:virial}) and does not
improve with resolution --- see the measured numbers in
\S\ref{sec:limitacoes}. A sequence ending is, in itself, a physical
result (not an error to be fixed): it signals the limit of validity of
that family of equilibria.

\textbf{M-R diagram.} $R_{eq}$ (km) on the x-axis, $M/M_\odot$ on the
y-axis, colored by $k_0$. The horizontal line at $1.44\,M_\odot$ marks
the Chandrasekhar limit \textbf{without a field} ($\mu_e=2$) --- it is
not the physical limit of the magnetized sequence, it's just a reading
reference. Optional overlay of literature data points from
\code{dashboard/data/references/bera\_bhattacharya\_2014.csv}, if the
file exists (it does not currently exist in this repository --- no data
has been digitized; the dashboard works without it and warns that it's
missing).

\begin{whybox}{Why the relevant mass maximum is over the whole plane, not a slice}
This is the point that most often causes an incorrect comparison with
the literature. $M_{max}(k_0{=}0)$ already depends on $\rho_c$: it only
approaches the Chandrasekhar limit asymptotically, for high $\rho_c$
($\sim10^{11}$--$10^{12}$ g/cm$^3$ --- see \code{scf/tests/test\_scf\_v1.py},
validated to $0.78\%$ in that range). A slice at low $\rho_c$ (for
example $\rho_c=10^9$, used during this project's debugging) gives
$M(k_0{=}0)=1.39\,M_\odot$ --- \textbf{already below} the field-free
Chandrasekhar limit, and any mass measured along that slice (with or
without a field) is not comparable to the literature reference number
($M_{max}\sim1.9\,M_\odot$ in Bera \& Bhattacharya 2014), which is the
maximum taken over the \textbf{entire} $(\rho_c,k_0)$ plane, not over a
line with $k_0$ varying at fixed $\rho_c$. This tab's sweep is exactly
what allows that comparison to be made correctly --- by mapping the
plane, not a slice.
\end{whybox}

\textbf{Grid convergence.} Points that fail to converge are recorded (not
silently dropped) and shown in a separate expander; the grid's
convergence rate is, itself, information about where the family of
solutions ends.

\textbf{VE heat map.} Over the $(\rho_c,k_0)$ grid, in
$\log_{10}(\mathrm{VE})$ --- reveals where the method is at the edge of
validity without having to read numbers one by one.

\section{Tab 3 --- Export}

\arrowto{dashboard/pages/3\_export.py}

Takes an already-saved equilibrium (Tab 1 or 2), imposes the toroidal
field (\S\ref{sec:toroidal}) and generates the three output artifacts:
an HDF5 initial data file, Castro's \code{inputs}, and
\code{run\_manifest.json}.

\textbf{Toroidal imposition.} Same function as \S\ref{sec:toroidal}
(\code{toroidal.solve\_zeta\_for\_energy\_ratio()}), with this tab's own
controls (the target ratio may differ from the one used when the
equilibrium was saved). Continuity of $\beta\beta'$ at the torus edge is
\textbf{guaranteed analytically} for $m\ge1$ (it is not recomputed
numerically here --- it is a property of the functional form of
\S\ref{sec:toroidal}, $(u-u_c)^{m+1}$ and its derivative go to zero at
$u=u_c$ for any $m\ge1$); the page only confirms that
\code{m\_tor}$\ge1$ was respected.

\textbf{Why the field is initialized from the vector potential, not from
B at cell centers.} This is a Castro-side decision (D3 of the plan,
section 5), not something the SCF resolves --- but the dashboard already
prepares the data in that format: the exported HDF5 file contains
$A_\varphi$ (computed as $u/\varpi$ from the converged flux function),
\textbf{not} $B_r$, $B_\theta$ directly. Castro's constrained-transport
algorithm keeps $\mathbf{B}$ on the mesh faces; initializing via
$\nabla\times\mathbf{A}$ interpolated onto the edges guarantees
$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0$ to machine precision by construction.
Initializing with $\mathbf{B}$ values at cell centers does not give that
guarantee. \textbf{What's missing:} interpolating $A_\varphi$ onto the
Cartesian mesh edges and computing $\nabla\times\mathbf{A}$ there is the
responsibility of Castro's \code{problem\_initialize\_mhd\_data.H}, which
has not been written yet (Phase 0 of the plan is pending) --- the
dashboard delivers $A_\varphi$ on a spherical $(r,\theta)$ mesh;
interpolation onto Castro's Cartesian mesh happens on the other side.

\textbf{$B'=B/\sqrt{4\pi}$ convention.} See \S1.10. The HDF5 file
exported by this tab stores $B_\varphi$ in pure gauss, with a
\code{units} attribute in the header saying so explicitly --- the
conversion to the Castro convention does not yet happen in this pipeline
(see the status note in \S1.10 and the gap in \S\ref{sec:aberto}).

\textbf{Derived scales and simulation cost.} $t_{dyn}$, $v_A$,
$t_{\text{Alfven}}$ and the $t_{\text{Alfven}}/t_{dyn}$ ratio from
\S1.10, computed from the $\langle B\rangle$ and $\bar\rho$ of the loaded
equilibrium. A success badge appears when the ratio is between 0.3 and 3
--- the strong-field regime that makes the simulation cheap (D4 of the
plan).

\textbf{Torus resolution check.} Given the number of cells per side of
Castro's box (128/256/384) and the box size (a multiple of $R_{eq}$),
computes how many cells cross the torus's radial extent
(\code{toroidal.torus\_radial\_extent()}, measured at the equator).
Fewer than 10 cells triggers a warning --- the torus is small compared
to the star and it is the torus that needs to be resolved (plan, section
6).

\textbf{Box parameters.} \code{castro.small\_dens} is chosen as
$\rho_c\times10^{-8}$ --- this is a \textbf{convention of this
dashboard's design}, not a value derived from physics nor cited in the
plan; there is no documented theoretical justification for the $10^{-8}$
exponent beyond it being a density floor low enough not to perturb the
star and high enough not to generate an absurd $v_A$ in the numerical
vacuum (the ``fluff problem'', plan section 5). The sponge start radius
($1.5\,R_{eq}$), the damping duration ($5\,t_{dyn}$) and the perturbation
amplitude ($10^{-4}c_s$, using \code{eos.sound\_speed()} at the center)
come directly from the values cited in the plan (section 5), and are not
recalculated/optimized by this dashboard.

\textbf{R5 --- export blocked if $\mathrm{VE}\ge10^{-3}$}, with no
override option. Implemented as a simple \code{if}/\code{else} around
the export button --- there is no override mechanism at any layer.

parameters. \code{git\_dirty()} is also recorded --- it flags whether
there were uncommitted changes at the time of the run, which makes exact
reproduction impossible even knowing the hash.

cache before the version guard existed.

\section{Known limitations}
\label{sec:limitacoes}

Measured numbers, not vague descriptions --- all in the $\rho_c=10^9$
g/cm$^3$, $R\approx3\times10^8$ cm, \code{nr=161}, \code{ntheta=65},
$l_{max}=16$ configuration unless otherwise noted:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.4em]
\item \textbf{The $\mathrm{VE}<10^{-3}$ criterion fails above
  $k_0\approx2\times10^{-12}$} in this configuration. The last valid
  point is $k_0\approx1.6\times10^{-12}$, $M\approx1.50\,M_\odot$,
  $\mathrm{VE}\approx6.6\times10^{-4}$. At $k_0\approx2.3\times10^{-12}$
  ($M\approx2.02\,M_\odot$), $\mathrm{VE}\approx1.57\times10^{-3}$ ---
  above the threshold.
\item \textbf{At that same point ($k_0\approx2.3\times10^{-12}$) the
  density peak migrates away from the center} --- from \code{r\_idx=1}
  (essentially the origin) to \code{r\_idx}$\approx25$ (a real radius,
  not a grid artifact) --- and $R_{pol}/R_{eq}$ drops to $\sim0.61$:
  genuine equatorial evacuation. The anchor at $\rho_c(r{=}0)$
  (\S\ref{sec:bernoulli}) loses physical meaning right there, because
  the center is no longer the point of maximum density.
\item \textbf{Whether the VE failure above is insufficient resolution or
  a genuine sequence termination is an open question --- but there is
  strong evidence for the second hypothesis}: a convergence study done
  in this project ($l_{max}$ from 16 to 48, mesh from $129^2$ to
  $385^2$, nearly $9\times$ more points) kept VE at
  $1.2$--$1.6\times10^{-3}$, \textbf{with no downward trend} --- a
  plateau above the threshold, not a curve converging to zero. This is
  the expected signature of a physical termination, not of
  under-resolution.
\item \textbf{All the sequence results ($k_0$ varying) documented above
  are from a single $\rho_c=10^9$ slice}, where
  $M(k_0{=}0)=1.39\,M_\odot$ --- below the field-free Chandrasekhar
  limit itself ($1.44\,M_\odot$). Comparisons with literature mass
  maxima ($M_{max}\sim1.9\,M_\odot$, Bera \& Bhattacharya 2014) require
  sweeping the entire $(\rho_c,k_0)$ plane (Tab 2), not a slice --- see
  \S3.
\item \textbf{Castro's $B'=B/\sqrt{4\pi}$ conversion is not applied
  anywhere in the current export pipeline} (\S1.10, \S4) --- the
  exported HDF5 file stores $B$ in pure gauss, documented via an
  attribute. This is left as the responsibility of Castro's
  \code{problem\_initialize.H} (not yet written).
\item \textbf{Phase 0 of the plan (building Castro with
  \code{USE\_MHD=TRUE}) is pending} --- system dependencies
  (\code{gfortran}, \code{libhdf5-openmpi-dev}, \code{libopenmpi-dev})
  have not yet been installed in this environment. Nothing in Tab 3 has
  been tested against a real Castro build.
\item \textbf{Rigid rotation (V-R1) does not validate against the
  literature ($\sim1.5\,M_\odot$)} --- the $\Omega_c$-controlled sequence
  terminates numerically at $R_{pol}/R_{eq}\approx0.93$ with mass-loss
  ratio only $0.135$ (should be $\to1$ at real breakup; the Roche model
  predicts $R_{pol}/R_{eq}=2/3$ for this EOS's central concentration).
  Not on the project's critical path (differential rotation, V-R2,
  already reaches the $\sim2.2\,M_\odot$ target at $0.40\%$ error, far
  from breakup) --- see \S\ref{sec:rotation}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Open questions}
\label{sec:aberto}

Gaps identified while writing this document --- not filled in on our own
initiative (rule G1):

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.35em]
\item \textbf{Flux consistency test}
  ($u(\varpi,z)=\int_0^\varpi B_z\varpi'\,d\varpi'$, \S\ref{sec:gs}) was
  proposed during debugging of the Green's-function bug but never
  implemented as a separate test. The Ampère test alone was enough to
  find and confirm the bug; the flux test would serve as a second,
  independent line of defense.
\item \textbf{Magnetic virial identity}
  ($\int\rho\nabla M(u)\cdot\mathbf{r}\,dV=\int B^2/8\pi\,dV$,
  \S\ref{sec:virial}) has no dedicated function in \code{diagnostics.py}
  and no committed test --- it was only verified numerically in an ad
  hoc way during one debugging session.
\item \textbf{The formula $J_\varphi=c\rho\varpi f(u)$} (\S\ref{sec:gs})
  does not correspond to any function --- it is never computed as a
  named quantity in the code.
\item \textbf{$B_{unit}=R\rho\sqrt{8\pi G}$} (\S1.10, natural field unit)
  is not implemented --- it was used only as an order-of-magnitude
  estimate during debugging.
\item \textbf{\code{schema\_version}} was missing --- \textbf{fixed}, see
  \S\ref{sec:aba4}.
\item \textbf{\code{store.mark\_reference()}} records the
  \code{reference} flag in the index, but no other tab (in particular
  Tab 2, which per the original prompt should show reference runs on its
  charts) reads that flag yet.
\item \textbf{\code{castro.small\_dens}$=\rho_c\times10^{-8}$} (\S4) is a
  convention with no documented theoretical justification --- it works
  as a starting point, not as a calibrated value.
\item \textbf{The original question that motivated the whole
  \S\ref{sec:gs} bug investigation} --- whether the ratio
  $(E_{mag}/|W|)/(M_u/H_c)\approx0.5$ (linear regime) has a closed-form
  derivation, or is just what is observed numerically for this EOS and
  this parameter range --- was not resolved analytically, only
  confirmed as self-consistent (constant under $k_0\to2k_0$).
\item \textbf{\code{gauss\_to\_castro()}/\code{castro\_to\_gauss()}}
  exist and are correct (checked in this work cycle), but are not called
  by any export code yet --- see the limitation in
  \S\ref{sec:limitacoes}.
\item \textbf{Rigid-rotation sequence termination} (\S\ref{sec:rotation},
  V-R1) --- not resolved. The standard remedy (parametrize the
  near-terminal sequence by axis ratio instead of $\Omega_c$, solving
  $\Omega_c$ by an outer root-find --- the technique Hachisu's own method
  uses) was identified and spot-checked (a direct bisection on axis ratio
  saturates at the same point found by stepping $\Omega_c$, confirming
  $\Omega_c$ degenerates as a control parameter there) but not
  implemented as production code --- out of scope for now since
  differential rotation (the project's actual target) does not hit this
  problem in the tested range.
\item \textbf{No domain-overflow detection}
  (\S\ref{sec:rho-bug-sweep}) --- nothing checks whether a computed
  $R_{eq}$/$R_{pol}$ has suspiciously landed at (or near) the outer edge
  of the computational domain, which is exactly the failure signature
  seen when chasing V-R1 (the SCF snapping to a spurious branch that
  fills the whole box). Not the same bug class as \code{surface\_radius}
  (this is a ``should this radius be trusted at all'' question, not an
  interpolation-precision one) --- surfaced by the same investigation,
  not implemented here.
\end{enumerate}
